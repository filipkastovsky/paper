Direction 02 — Editorial

A quiet kind of money app.

What if the Duolingo-for-crypto didn't shout, but whispered? This direction borrows the visual grammar of a design journal — Gloock for masthead, Schibsted Grotesk for copy, ink on warm paper, one single terracotta accent. It's still friendly, still beginner-safe — but it treats the reader like someone with taste.

Design — Claude × Filip Subject — Neo-Fintech v0 Date — 2026.04.23

Why serif

A high-contrast display serif makes numerals feel considered rather than volatile. Balances read as statements, not tickers — the exact tone we want for paper trading.

The single accent

One terracotta hue, nowhere else. Reserved for kickers, the italicised headline emphasis, and the Buy button. Used everywhere, it stops working.

What it trades off

Less Instagram-friendly than pastel — but more Tumblr-friendly. Attracts a slightly older, design-literate audience. Good for a slow brand, risky for a viral one.

i. Palette

The whole system is built on two warm cream papers, one deep ink, and exactly one accent. Greens and warning oranges derive from the accent hue to maintain chromatic discipline. Nothing sings; everything holds its tune.

ii. Typography

Gloock handles all display — mastheads, balances, headlines. It's a modern, contrasty serif with the right amount of personality at huge sizes. Schibsted Grotesk (a free release from Norwegian media house Schibsted) carries body copy, metadata, and UI chrome.

iii. Screens

The same four load-bearing moments — onboarding balance reveal, dashboard, trade, share card — but treated like editorial spreads. Rules replace cards. Hierarchy comes from type, never from tint or shadow.
